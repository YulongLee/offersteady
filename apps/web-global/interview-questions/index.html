What are behavioural interview questions evaluating?

Behavioural questions examine how you acted in a real situation: your ownership, judgement, communication, conflict handling, prioritisation, or learning. Choose an example where your contribution is clear. Explain the context briefly, describe the decisions you personally made, and connect the result to the capability the role requires.

If you cannot verify a metric, describe the observable outcome without inventing precision. A credible limitation is stronger than an impressive unsupported claim.

How should you answer technical concept questions?

Begin with a precise definition, then explain the mechanism and why it matters. Compare alternatives using relevant trade-offs such as consistency, latency, cost, complexity, security, or operability. Finish with a real example or failure mode when you have one.

When the question is ambiguous, state your interpretation and ask a clarifying question. This demonstrates scope control and prevents an answer to the wrong problem.

What is a good coding-question workflow?

Restate the problem, confirm inputs and outputs, identify constraints, and test a simple example. Explain a straightforward solution before optimising. Choose data structures deliberately, state time and space complexity, then test edge cases such as empty input, duplicates, limits, overflow, or invalid state.

Generated code is a draft. Confirm the requested language, syntax, library assumptions, and all hidden constraints before using it.

How should you approach a system-design question?

Clarify the users, core actions, scale, latency, consistency, availability, security, and cost expectations before drawing components. Define the main interfaces and data flow, then explain storage, caching, asynchronous work, failure handling, and observability at the level the interviewer requests.

There is rarely one correct architecture. State assumptions and compare alternatives. If the interviewer changes a constraint, identify which part of the design is affected and why instead of replacing the whole answer without explanation.

How do you handle a question you cannot answer fully?

State what you understand, identify the missing fact, and ask a focused clarification. If you know a related mechanism, explain the connection without pretending it is the same answer. A transparent boundary gives the interviewer a useful view of your reasoning and avoids an unsupported claim.

For an unfamiliar tool, reason from the problem it appears to solve and compare it with a system you know. Separate verified knowledge from your inference. OfferSteady guidance can also be incomplete, so apply the same standard to anything shown in the workspace.

How do follow-up questions change the answer?

A follow-up usually introduces a new constraint or asks for evidence behind an earlier claim. Address the changed condition first, preserve relevant context, and avoid repeating the full original response. If your earlier assumption is no longer valid, say so and explain the revised decision.

Listen for the exact dimension being tested. A request for more detail is different from disagreement, a new scale assumption, or a request for a concrete example. Confirm the change and then update only the affected part of your reasoning.

AI output is guidance. Verify every claim, answer from your real experience, and follow the interview organiser's rules.
