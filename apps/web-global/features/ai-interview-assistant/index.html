What is an AI interview assistant?

An AI interview assistant is a tool that helps a candidate interpret questions, organise evidence, and prepare a clear response. OfferSteady supports this workflow with selected resumes, job descriptions, knowledge materials, typed questions, realtime transcripts, and screenshots. It produces guidance rather than speaking for the candidate or guaranteeing an outcome.

The most useful output is specific enough to organise an answer but cautious enough to preserve the candidate's judgement. That is why OfferSteady asks users to verify every claim and keep answers faithful to their actual responsibilities, decisions, results, and lessons.

How does session context work?

You choose the material for each interview before the live session starts. A resume can establish the experience you want to discuss, a job description can identify the role's priorities, and a knowledge collection can add selected project or domain details. Nothing should be treated as verified merely because it appears in a generated response.

Separating session context also reduces irrelevant material. If the role changes, create or update the session selection rather than assuming that one answer profile fits every interview.

When should you use manual or automatic answers?

Manual Quick Answer keeps the candidate in control: confirm that a real interviewer question has been recognised, then request guidance. Auto Answer can react to confirmed interviewer questions when enabled, but it remains optional and can be turned off. Typed questions are useful when audio is unavailable or when a clarification needs to be entered directly.

Whichever mode you choose, read the suggestion critically. Correct factual errors, remove claims you cannot support, and use the structure as a prompt for your own response.

How do you prepare evidence for a session?

Read the job description and identify a small set of responsibilities that matter for this interview. For each one, choose an example you can explain without notes: the situation, your decision, the work you personally completed, the result, and what you learned. Update the selected resume or knowledge material when a fact is missing rather than expecting the model to infer it.

Keep source material focused. A concise project summary with accurate names, dates, constraints, and outcomes is more useful than a large collection of unrelated documents. Do not add confidential employer information or personal data that the interview does not require.

How should generated guidance be adapted?

Start with the direct answer, then choose only the evidence relevant to the question. Replace generic phrases with details you can verify and remove recommendations that do not match your role. If the question asks for an opinion, explain the principle and trade-off behind your choice instead of presenting one approach as universally correct.

A suggestion may be too long for a live conversation. Use the opening and key evidence as a speaking outline, listen for follow-up questions, and answer those follow-ups directly. The goal is a clearer version of your own reasoning, not a script that hides uncertainty.

Responsible-use boundary

Interview rules differ by employer, assessment, and jurisdiction. Confirm whether AI assistance, recording, transcription, or screen capture is permitted. Do not upload trade secrets, unrelated personal data, or material you do not have the right to process.

AI output is guidance. Verify every claim, answer from your real experience, and follow the interview organiser's rules.
